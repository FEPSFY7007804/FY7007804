The Evolution of Starship

    

How SpaceX went from a water-tower-shaped test hopper to the largest and most powerful rocket ever flown, and why it matters for the Moon and Mars.

  
     
    Section 1     

What is Starship?

    

Starship is a two-stage rocket built by SpaceX. The first stage, called Super Heavy, is a giant booster powered by 33 Raptor engines. The second stage, also called Starship, is the spacecraft that carries crew or cargo.

    

What makes it different from almost every rocket before it is that both stages are designed to be fully reusable. Most rockets are thrown away after one flight, which is like building a new aeroplane for every journey. If Starship can be reused like an aircraft, the cost of reaching space could fall dramatically.

  
     
    Section 2     

Key milestones

    
          
  • 2019 – Starhopper. A stubby test vehicle with a single Raptor engine made short hops of up to about 150 m at Boca Chica, Texas, proving the engine could lift and land a vehicle.
    •       
  • December 2020 – SN8. The first high-altitude test of a full-size Starship prototype. It performed the famous "belly flop" manoeuvre but exploded on landing. SpaceX treated this as a success because it gathered huge amounts of data.
    •       
  • May 2021 – SN15. After several more crashes, SN15 became the first prototype to fly to high altitude and land in one piece.
    •       
  • April 2023 – Integrated Flight Test 1. The first launch of the full Super Heavy and Starship stack. It cleared the pad but tumbled and was destroyed a few minutes into flight.
    •       
  • June 2024 – Flight 4. Both the booster and the ship made controlled splashdowns in the ocean for the first time.
    •       
  • October 2024 – Flight 5. The Super Heavy booster flew back to the launch tower and was caught in mid-air by the tower's "chopstick" arms, a world first.
    •     
  
     
    Chart 1     

How big is Starship?

    

Fully stacked, Starship stands taller than Saturn V, the rocket that took astronauts to the Moon, and taller than Big Ben's Elizabeth Tower.

    
  
     
    Chart 2     

The Raptor engine keeps getting stronger

    

Each new version of the Raptor engine produces more thrust while becoming simpler and lighter. Raptor 3 removes many external pipes and parts, making it easier to build and reuse.

    
  
     
    Section 3     

Why it matters

    

Back to the Moon: NASA chose a version of Starship as the Human Landing System for its Artemis programme, which aims to land astronauts on the Moon for the first time since 1972.

    

Mars: SpaceX's long-term goal is to use Starship to carry people and cargo to Mars. This depends on refuelling Starship in orbit, which is still being developed.

    

Engineering approach: SpaceX uses rapid "build, fly, fail, fix" testing. Instead of years of design before a single flight, they fly prototypes early and learn from each failure.
